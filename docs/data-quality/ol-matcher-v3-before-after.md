# Open Library matcher v3: before and after, on list-style queries

**Measured 2026-10-05** against the 2026-07-31 Open Library artifact
(`/home/shane/ol-data/versions/2026-07-31/`), on the expanded 598-case labelled set: the 448
existing cases with duplicate-aware labels, plus 150 new `list_row` cases. Regenerate before
acting; the numbers describe a moment.

- Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-04-ol-matcher-list-queries-design.md`
- Before: `data-sources/src/openlibrary/eval/readings/2026-10-v2-baseline.json` (matcher 2, the
  shipped weights, the new labels)
- After: `data-sources/src/openlibrary/eval/readings/2026-10-v3-after.json` (matcher 3,
  recalibrated weights, the same labels)

The two readings use the same cases and the same labels, so every difference between them comes
from the matcher. One exception: the baseline was taken before commit `59ffe9c0`, which removed
alternate `OL45820336W` from `list_row-089`. That case abstains in both readings, so no metric
moves. The tables at the bottom of this file are generated by `openlibrary.eval.compare`
(see "Regenerating"). Everything above them was written by hand, as was the "hand review" column
of the decision diff.

108 decisions changed: 89 newly accepted, 19 newly abstained. No decision became a reject, and
no accept moved to a different key. Every one of the 89 new accepts was read by hand against the
artifact: **none is a wrong book**. Three picked a non-canonical duplicate of the right book

List-row abstention fell by 45%, not the 50% the spec expected. The remaining 76 abstains are
explained below. In 70 of them the right work is already ranked first. 61 abstain on the
margin, and 9 of those would have been accepted at v2's margin threshold (0.175). The
recalibration raised it to 0.204, which also holds back two false merges on the rest of the
set.

**One risk to know before merging.** Those two would-be false merges are generic titles by
prolific authors (*George Washington*, *X-Men*). Their margins sit 0.001 and 0.009 below the
threshold. Two false merges would still pass the global 0.015 gate, so only this report records
how close they are. See "The false merges the margin holds back".

Three strata lost more than one correct case. None is a code defect. The `non_latin_title`
losses do point at a gap worth a follow-up (see "Strata that lost more than one correct case").

The Rails replay (spec item 5) leaves the finder correct on 195 of 200 rows in both runs, and
Open Library's accepts rise from 7 to 80. None of the 10 accepts that differ from our stored key
is a wrong book. They do expose a hazard for anything that creates books from these answers. A
local copy of the work can hold a duplicate or redirected key that the finder never looks up,
so an import creates a second copy. See "The hazard: an accept no local book holds".

## Hand review of the decision diff

Every `newly_accepted` row (89) was read against the artifact through the v3 API's
`POST /works/batch`. For each row the reviewer compared the query's title and authors with the
accepted work's title, authors, edition count and first year, read the label's rationale, and
read the labelled work as well where it differed from the accepted one. There are no
`key_changed` rows. The verdict for each row is in the "hand review" column of the generated
table. The 19 `newly_abstained` rows carry "n/a (no accept after)": an abstain cannot be a wrong
book. They are covered in the next two sections.

- **Wrong books: none.**
- **Non-canonical duplicates (3):**
  - `isbn_reuse-014`, Eisler's *The Chalice and the Blade*. It accepted `OL271806W` (8 editions)
    over the labelled `OL2041228W` (5 editions). Both are the book. The accepted record is
    arguably the better canonical choice.
  - `list_row-022`, Momaday's *The Names*. It accepted `OL276931W` ("Names", 2 editions). The
    canonical `OL25929W` is mistitled "Why did you do it" after one stray edition, so no title
    comparison can reach it.
  - `list_row-068`, Murakami's *The Wind-Up Bird Chronicle*. It accepted `OL25111252W`, the
    2-edition English record. The canonical `OL2625412W` is the 34-edition Japanese record, whose
    title fingerprints to nothing comparable.
- **Duplicates lists hold more than duplicates.** `decision.duplicates` lists every other member
  of the winning cluster, and a cluster can hold a different text that shares the title and an
  author. Two of the new accepts show it. `list_row-038`'s list includes `OL20168372W`, Fred
  Fordham's graphic-novel adaptation of *To Kill a Mockingbird* (Open Library stores "A Graphic
  Novel" as its subtitle, so its title fingerprint is exactly "to kill a mockingbird", and Harper
  Lee is credited). `list_row-066`'s list includes `OL34523825W`, a 708-page
  Italian omnibus of *Hyperion* and *The Fall of Hyperion*. The accepted representative is right
  in both cases, and the field is evidence only today: no Rails code reads it. Spec 3 (the
  backfill of duplicate keys) must verify each entry before stamping it, not copy the list.

## Strata that lost more than one correct case

The spec requires any stratum that lost more than one correct case to be investigated before
merging. Each lost case was re-scored from the prepared caches, under both matchers, to see
which features moved.

| Stratum | Correct before | Correct after | Lost cases |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `anthology_or_collection` | 7 | 4 | `-007`, `-018`, `-028` |
| `author_less_work` | 11 | 9 | `-001`, `-003` |
| `non_latin_title` | 4 | 1 | `-018`, `-020`, `-030` |

Seven of the eight were accepts that v2 made with a score barely over the 0.9 accept threshold
(0.900 to 0.925). The title evidence behind them was perfect only by accident. In every one of
them our identifiers reached the work: the right answer came from the identifier, and the title
was close enough not to block it. v3 measures titles more strictly and the recalibration weighs
an inexact title more heavily: `title_variant_exact` went from 0.33 to 0.64. Each of these cases
now scores 0.71 to 0.85 and lands in the middle band.

**`anthology_or_collection` (7 to 4).**
- `-007`, *The Selected Poetry of Alexander Pope*. The work's stored title is the catalogue
  inversion "Pope, The Selected Poetry of Alexander". Word-order-blind similarity is still 1.0,
  but no variant is an exact match, and the heavier exact-title weight moves the score from 0.900
  to 0.842. Cause: the recalibrated weights. Accepted cost.
- `-018`, Frost's *Collected Poems, Prose, And Plays*. The stored title is "Collected poems,
  prose & plays". The fingerprint does not treat "&" and "and" as the same word, and v2's
  `token_set_ratio` hid that (1.0). v3's `token_sort_ratio` scores 0.931, and with the weights the
  case scores 0.836. Cause: the title comparison together with the weights. This is a
  normalisation gap rather than a v3 bug: the same "&" problem costs two list rows (see below).
  Possible follow-up.
- `-028`, Weldon Kees, *Collected Poems*. The labelled work is titled "The collected poems of
  Weldon Kees". v2 scored the subset as a perfect title (defect D1). v3 scores 0.667, by design,
  and calibration left `subset_title_credit` at 0.0. Another Kees work is titled exactly
  "Collected poems" (`OL4625700W`, with an identifier conflict). Abstaining between the two is
  the right call. Cause: the title comparison working as intended. Accepted cost.

**`author_less_work` (11 to 9).**
- `-001`, *Poems of Thomas Hood*. The labelled work is "Poems by Thomas Hood" (73 editions), and
  title similarity fell from 0.919 to 0.85 (score 0.846). Four other Hood works are titled
  exactly "Poems of Thomas Hood", all with identifier conflicts. Cause: the title comparison.
  Abstaining is defensible. Accepted cost.
- `-003`, *The Jewel in the Lotus*. The only margin loss: margin 0.154, below the 0.204
  threshold. The runner-up `OL9679561W` ("Jewel In the Lotus", the stored key) is the same book.
  Its author record is garbled ("Allen Edwardes introduction Albert Ellis"), so it shares no
  author fingerprint with the winner and cannot join its cluster. It also carries our
  identifiers. v3's article-stripped variant makes its title exact, which lifts it from 0.754 to
  0.801. The margin would fail at v2's 0.175 too. Cause: the title variants plus a bad OL author
  record. Accepted cost: abstaining between two records of the same book is safe.

**`non_latin_title` (4 to 1).** All three are manga volumes. The query mixes Japanese with a
romanised bracket ("乙嫁語り 6 [Otoyomegatari 6]"), and the work's title is Japanese only
("乙嫁語り 6巻"). The stored fingerprint keeps only the Latin leftovers of the work title, here
the volume number "6". v2's `token_set_ratio` scored that shared digit as a perfect 1.0 title
(defect D1 again). v3 scores 0.08 to 0.11, and the identifier alone cannot lift the score past
0.9 (0.715 to 0.735). Cause: the title comparison. The v2 accepts were right only because our
ISBNs reached the work.

This is **not a bug against the spec**: a non-empty fingerprint counts as comparable, and only an
empty one is absent (R40). It is the one place where v3 is measurably worse for a reason worth
fixing, though. A lossy fingerprint (the `_lossy` test Ruling 9 added for clustering: an
alphabetic character outside a-z after accents are stripped) is scored as title disagreement
when it is really missing evidence. Scoring it as absent, when an identifier agrees, would
recover these three and `high_frequency_title-009` ("Vagabond, Volume 2" against
"バガボンド(2)") without loosening anything else. R58 would still demand identity evidence, and
the identifier provides it. Not done here: it is a matcher change and needs its own measurement.

### Margin (61)

Grouped by what the runner-up is:

| Margin | Runner-up | Rows |
|---|---|---:|
| below 0.07 | Same author, with a stored title that is the query plus a tail | 23 |
| 0.10 to 0.175 | A record with **no author at all** and the same title | 26 |
| 0.10 to 0.175 | Same author, different or variant title | 3 |
| 0.175 to 0.204 | Same author, different or misspelled title | 9 |

**Below 0.07: a title plus a tail (23 rows, margins 0.010 to 0.061).** The runner-up is by the
same author, and its stored title is the query's title followed by something: a subtitle, a
series tag, an edition note, or other contents. Examples: "The Big Time; the Mind Spider and
Other Stories", "THE JOY LUCK CLUB (film tie-in)", "Tree Grows in Brooklyn (SparkNotes
Literature Guide)", "Ringworld : The Graphic Novel, Part Two", "The Haunting of Hill House (A
Play)", "Boneshaker (FIRST HARDCOVER EDITION!)", "Middlemarch (100 Copy Limited Edition)".

The work's stored no-subtitle variant (`title_fp_nosub`, cut at the first `:`, `;` or `(`)
equals the query exactly. So the runner-up scores 1.0 on title similarity and 1.0 on
`title_variant_exact`, the same as the real work. Only popularity separates them, and popularity
is worth at most about 0.06. The spec's `DERIVED_TITLE_FACTOR` (0.95) caps a subtitle the
*query* drops. Nothing caps a subtitle the *work* drops.

They do not cluster because Ruling 7 deliberately keeps the subtitle-stripped variant out of
cluster membership. That is right: roughly half of these runner-ups are a different text (an
omnibus, a study guide, a play, a graphic novel, a single volume). The other half are the same
book under a junk title. Two of them are labelled duplicates: `list_row-037` "Lake Wobegon Days"
and `list_row-085` "Will in the World How Shakespeare Became Shakespeare". Abstaining is the
right outcome here until the matcher can tell those two kinds apart. Lowering the margin would
not help: these margins sit far below any plausible threshold.

**0.10 to 0.175: an authorless same-title record (26 rows).** The runner-up is an Open Library
work with no author at all and the identical title: "The Forever War", "Middlesex", "Lord of
the Rings", "American Gods" and so on. Absence is neutral (R40), so a record with only a title
scores about 0.816 on title and popularity alone, against about 0.95 for the real work. Most of
these records are probably stub duplicates of the book, but without an author nothing can show
it. Clustering needs a shared author fingerprint, so they never join the winner's cluster. Even
v2's 0.175 margin would not have accepted any of these rows (the largest margin is 0.173).

Whether a candidate with no author evidence should be allowed to set the margin when the query
names an author is a policy question. In spirit it is the same question identity-first ranking
answered for title-less candidates: a candidate that cannot confirm the author is weak evidence
against one that can. Not changed here.

**0.10 to 0.204: same author, a different or variant title (12 rows).** Nine have margins
between 0.176 and 0.197. Their runner-ups are other books by the same author ("Children of
Strife" for *Children of Time*, "Parable of the Sower" for *Parable of the Talents*), a
translation ("Asymmetrie"), misspelled duplicates ("Hiroshim", "Lone Some Dove ", "Css
Revolution in the Head") and parts or proofs ("Untold Stories Pt. 1", "The Uncertain Places A
Novel", "Dark Back of Time Proof ( Publicity Only)"). All nine would be accepted at v2's margin
threshold, 0.175, and each of those accepts would be right. See "Calibration" for why the
threshold rose. The other three sit lower: `list_row-106` (runner-up "More Tales of the City",
0.172), `list_row-017` (runner-up "Metaphor & memory", a labelled duplicate the "&" gap keeps
out of the cluster, 0.160) and `list_row-031` (0.141), described next.

The single margin row whose top candidate is wrong is `list_row-031`, *The Autobiography of
Malcolm X*. The top candidate is a SparkNotes guide ("Autobiography of Malcolm X (SparkNotes
Literature Guide)", credited to Alex Haley). The labelled work is the 70-edition record stored
under its Spanish title, "Autobiografía Malcolm X", which ranks second with margin 0.141. At a
margin threshold below 0.141 this row becomes a false merge. That is the same title-plus-tail
weakness, seen from the other side.

### What would move the rest

By cause. None of these is in this branch, and each needs its own measurement.

1. The objective, or the margin. Nine rows sit between 0.175 and 0.204. See "Calibration".
2. Authorless runner-ups setting the margin: 26 rows. A policy question, discussed above.
3. Title plus a tail: 23 rows. This needs the matcher to tell a junk subtitle from different

4. "&" against "and" in the fingerprint: 2 list rows (`-133`, and `-017` *Metaphor & Memory*,

   fingerprint change affects stored fingerprints, so it needs `NORMALIZER_VERSION` and an

## Calibration

`calibrate --base` started from the shipped v2 vector under v3 semantics (TEST objective 0.9333)
and found a vector scoring 0.9383 on TEST and 0.9388 on TRAIN. The write gate accepted it.

|---|---:|---:|
| `title_similarity` | 1.000 | 1.051 |
| `title_variant_exact` | 0.327 | 0.645 |
| `subtitle_agreement` | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| `author_overlap` | 1.000 | 0.971 |
| `author_name_similarity` | 1.771 | 1.556 |
| `year_agreement` | 0.780 | 0.501 |
| `identifier_agreement` | 1.311 | 1.700 |
| `popularity_prior` | 0.281 | 0.412 |

| `subset_title_credit` (new) | -- | 0.0 |
| `duplicate_dominance_ratio` (new) | -- | 1.5 (the search floor) |

The search's largest single gain (step 151, objective 0.861 to 0.936) came from raising
`margin_threshold`. Under v3's margin semantics, the old 0.175 left one error on the training
split. `subset_title_credit` never moved in 2,000 steps: containment credit does nothing on this
set. `duplicate_dominance_ratio` finished on the floor of its search range (1.5), so the best
value may lie below the range searched.

The objective prices an abstention at 0.1, against 1 for a false reject and 10 for a false merge.
It has little reason to accept more once false merges are at zero.

At v2's margin, list-row abstention would clear the spec's halving expectation (0.447). The price

merges above the baseline's zero). The shipped value is the only row in this table that meets
every criterion except the halving.

### The false merges the margin holds back

The two cases the margin holds back are generic titles by prolific authors. Their margins sit very
close to the threshold:

- `high_frequency_title-034`, Freeman's *George Washington* (labelled `ambiguous`: our row mixes a
  one-volume abridgment with single volumes of the seven-volume original). Margin **0.203**
  against 0.204. Five same-title Freeman records cluster, and the representative would be
  accepted.
- `shared_key_collision-036`, Claremont's *X-Men: The Dark Phoenix Saga*. Margin **0.195**. Different
  Claremont collections all titled "X-Men" cluster as "duplicates": 18 members in the prepared
  cache behind reading 8 (counts through the live API can differ slightly). The dominance rule
  picks `OL461810W` because it has 14 editions against 7 for the next-largest member,
  `OL14928020W`, which clears the 1.5 dominance ratio. That 7-edition record is credited to Louise Simonson (and Louise Jones)
  only. It shares no author with the query. It joined the cluster through a co-credited record,
  `OL25324064W` (Claremont and Simonson among others). Clusters are connected components, so
  authors chain from one member to the next, and that chaining is part of the risk. The pick is
  not the Dark Phoenix Saga. This is the spec's named risk ("Clustering merges two different
  books") actually happening. The margin catches it today, with 0.009 to spare.

**Neither is a v3 false merge, but both pass the global gate if they ever appear.** Two false
merges in about 230 accepts is 0.009, under the 0.015 bound. Only the list-row false-merge gate
is zero, and these are not list rows. A future recalibration that lowers `margin_threshold`
below about 0.203 would bring back at least the first one, and nothing in CI or the build gate would
notice. One way to guard it: pin these two cases in `test_eval_regression.py` as "must not
accept".

### The hazard: an accept no local book holds

Only one of the 10 accepted keys is held by a local book (`OL2732491W`, by 5743). For the
other nine, the finder's identifier rules (1 and 2) cannot fire, even though in all nine our
linked book is the accepted work. In eight it holds a duplicate or redirected key, and in
*Rosewater* a wrong one. Two things keep the rules from seeing the first eight. `OpenLibrarySource#local_holders`
looks up only the accepted key and the record's `redirected_from`, never `decision.duplicates`.
And `/resolve` candidates always carry an empty `redirected_from` (it is filled only for the key
a `/works` request named), so the redirect branch never fires on resolve output.

What happens next depends on the finder's answer. Rule 5 answers `unmatched`, carrying the
accepted work, when there is no local candidate at all. The AI can also answer `unmatched` while
Open Library accepts. Either way, `ImporterBase#call` creates a new book for an `unmatched` match.
`Providers::OpenLibrary` reuses the finder's resolution for it and, on an accept, stamps the
accepted key. The result is a second copy of a book we already hold, stamped with a key that our
original does not carry.

**In this replay that happened once: *My Kind of Place* (40946).** The finder answered
`unmatched high` through the AI, with no review flag. An import would create a second *My Kind
of Place* stamped `OL14860213W` next to book 2221, whose `OL14860219W` redirects to that same
work. Rule 5 itself fired on no row. The other eight rows with an unheld accepted key were saved
only by rule 4's exact match on normalized title and author. A list row with a subtitle, a
variant spelling or a different name form sends the same book to the AI, or to rule 5 if neither
the exact lookup nor OpenSearch finds it. *The City in History* shows the same pattern: our key
`OL1879248W` redirects to the work Open Library ranked first. That row avoided the stamp only
because the service abstained, and the finder's `unmatched` would still create a new book.

**What the books list wizard (spec 2) must do:** before it creates a book from an `unmatched`
answer that carries an accept, it must treat a local book holding the accepted key, **any**
`decision.duplicates` key, or any key that redirects to one of those as a match. It should
send the row to review rather than create. The `duplicates` check alone would have caught 2 of
the 8 same-book rows here. The redirect check catches the other 6, and `/resolve` does not
supply what it needs: the wizard would have to resolve the local keys (for example with
`/works/batch`) or have the service return incoming redirects. `duplicates` is evidence, not
proof (see "Duplicate clusters" in the feature doc), so a holder found that way should go to
review rather than be merged automatically. This task changed no Rails code.

```ruby
# Replays the 200 spike rows through the Rails book finder and OL /resolve.
# Writes NOTHING: every finder call runs in a transaction that is rolled back.
# Makes AI calls (the finder's candidate picker): a few cents per run.
require "json"
out = ENV.fetch("OUT")

def split_authors(value)
  Array(value).flat_map { |s| s.to_s.split(/\s*;\s*|\s+&\s+|\s+and\s+/i) }.map(&:strip).reject(&:blank?)
end

items = ListItem.joins(:list).where(lists: {type: "Books::List"})
  .where("list_items.metadata ? 'title'").where.not(listable_id: nil)
  .order(Arel.sql("md5(list_items.id::text)")).limit(200).includes(:listable).to_a
client = Books::OpenLibrary::Client.new

File.open(out, "w") do |f|
  items.each_with_index do |li, i|
    title = li.metadata["title"].to_s
    authors = split_authors(li.metadata["authors"])
    label_keys = Identifier.where(identifiable: li.listable, identifier_type: :books_work_openlibrary_id).pluck(:value)
    row = {id: li.id, title: title, authors: authors, label_id: li.listable_id, label_ol: label_keys}
    begin
      ActiveRecord::Base.transaction do
        match = DataImporters::Books::Book::Finder.new.call(
          query: DataImporters::Books::Book::ImportQuery.new(title: title, author_names: authors)
        )
        row[:finder] = {outcome: match.outcome, confidence: match.confidence, decided_by: match.decided_by,
                        record_id: match.record&.id, correct: match.record&.id == li.listable_id,
                        needs_review: match.needs_review?, reason: match.reason.to_s[0, 300]}
        raise ActiveRecord::Rollback
      end
    rescue => e
      row[:finder_error] = "#{e.class}: #{e.message[0, 200]}"
    end
    begin
      r = client.resolve(title: title, author_names: authors)
      row[:ol] = {verdict: r.decision.verdict, key: r.decision.key, score: r.decision.score,
                  margin: r.decision.margin, reason: r.decision.reason.to_s[0, 200],
                  agrees_with_stored_key: label_keys.any? ? label_keys.include?(r.decision.key) : nil}
    rescue => e
      row[:ol_error] = "#{e.class}: #{e.message[0, 200]}"
    end
    f.puts(row.to_json)
    warn "#{i + 1}/#{items.size}" if ((i + 1) % 20).zero?
  end
end
```
